# Exposé V §§7–9 (gentle): the étale fundamental group of a scheme

## What this file is about

The fundamental group of a doughnut is easy to draw and impossible to argue with. The fundamental group of a system of
polynomial equations over a field of finite characteristic has no loops to draw at all, no continuous deformations to
consider, and no obvious reason to exist. This file constructs it anyway.

The construction takes the place of loops with the next best thing — the unbranched finite covers of the object — and
extracts a group from the way those covers fit together. Over the complex numbers the group built this way agrees, in a
precise sense, with the classical fundamental group of the underlying shape. Over other fields it gives an answer where
no shape was available to begin with. This is the companion Grothendieck wrote the rest of the seminar to build, and it
is the destination toward which the previous five files have been moving.

## Opening: the geometric payoff

The Galois-category framework of files 30–32 has been abstract. This file lands it. Take

```text
𝒞(S)  :=  category of finite étale coverings of a connected
          locally noetherian prescheme S,
F_a   :=  geometric fibre at a chosen geometric point
          a : Spec(Ω) → S of S.
```

The axioms (G1)–(G6) all hold (verifications below using the results of files 30 and 31), so V.4.1 yields a profinite
group

```text
π₁(S, a)  :=  Aut(F_a)
```

and an equivalence of categories `𝒞(S) ≃ 𝒞(π₁(S, a))`. This is the **étale fundamental group of `S` at `a`**, the
algebraic generalisation of the topological `π̂₁^top(B, b)` of file 28.

§7 makes this construction. §8 specialises to two important cases: the spectrum of a field (V.8.1:
`π₁(Spec k, ā) = Gal(k̄/k)`), and a normal base prescheme (V.8.2: the surjection `π₁(η) ↠ π₁(S)` from the function-field
point with kernel generated by ramification). §9 extends to non-connected `S` via multi-Galois categories.

The forward-references that have been carrying water since file 05 — the absolute Galois group sidebar, the étale
fundamental group note at the end of file 06, the transcendental computation aside in file 22, the four-exposé arc
closing file 27 — are resolved here.

> **Galois-category axioms (G1)–(G6), at a glance** (cf. file 31, §V.4). A pair `(𝒞, F)` of a category `𝒞` and a functor
> `F : 𝒞 → FSet` to finite sets is a **Galois category** when:
>
> - **(G1)** `𝒞` has a final object and admits fibre products (equivalently, finite projective limits);
> - **(G2)** `𝒞` has finite sums and quotients by finite groups of automorphisms (hence an initial object `∅_𝒞`);
> - **(G3)** every morphism `u : X → Y` factors as a strict epimorphism followed by a monomorphism onto a direct summand
>     of `Y`;
> - **(G4)** `F` is left exact: preserves the final object and fibre products;
> - **(G5)** `F` commutes with finite sums, sends strict epimorphisms to epimorphisms (i.e. surjections), and commutes
>     with quotients by finite groups of automorphisms;
> - **(G6)** `F` is conservative: if `F(u)` is an isomorphism in `FSet` then `u` is an isomorphism in `𝒞`.

## §V.7 The case of preschemes

Let `S` be a locally noetherian and **connected** prescheme. Fix a **geometric point** of `S`:

```text
a : Spec(Ω) ⟶ S,
```

with `Ω` an algebraically closed field. By definition, this is a morphism with `Ω` algebraically closed; equivalently,
an ordinary point `s ∈ S` together with an algebraic closure of `κ(s)` (more generally an algebraically closed
extension).

Set

```text
𝒞 := 𝒞(S)  =  category of finite étale coverings of S,
```

with morphisms = `S`-morphisms. For `X ∈ 𝒞`, set

```text
F(X) := F_a(X)  =  set of geometric points of X lying over a,
```

i.e. `Hom_S(Spec(Ω), X)`. This is a finite set (since `X / S` is finite étale and `Ω` algebraically closed: étale gives
`X ×_S Spec(Ω)` discrete and finite over `Spec(Ω)`).

### Verification of (G1)–(G6) for `(𝒞(S), F_a)`

The axioms hold from prior results:

- **(G1)** Final object exists (`S` itself) and fibre products exist in `𝒞(S)` — both are sorites of I.4.6 (file 11):
    finite étale is preserved under fibre product.
- **(G2)** Finite sums (disjoint union of étale covers) and quotients by finite groups of automorphisms exist — V.3.4
    (file 31).
- **(G3)** Strict-epi + monomorphism factorisation — V.3.5 + V.3.6 (file 31).
- **(G4)** `F_a` left exact — preserves final object (`F_a(S)` is one geometric point) and fibre products
    (`F_a(X ×_S Y) = F_a(X) ×_{F_a(S)} F_a(Y) = F_a(X) × F_a(Y)` since `F_a(S)` is a singleton).
- **(G5)** `F_a` carries finite sums to disjoint unions, strict epis to surjections, and quotients to quotients — V.3.5
    \+ the start of V.2 (file 30).
- **(G6)** `F_a` is conservative — V.3.7 (file 31): a morphism of étale coverings of a connected base is an iso iff it
    is iso on geometric fibres.

Theorem V.4.1 (file 31) therefore applies and produces:

- A pro-object `P = (P_i)_{i ∈ I}` of `𝒞(S)` representing `F_a`. This is the **universal cover of `S` at `a`**: a
    pro-finite- étale cover of `S` whose geometric fibre at `a` is `Aut(P) = Aut(F_a)`.

- A profinite group

    ```text
    π₁(S, a)  :=  Aut(F_a)  =  Aut(P)^{op},
    ```

    the **étale fundamental group of `S` at `a`**. Profinite by construction (V.4 step h).

- An equivalence of categories

    ```text
    F_a : 𝒞(S)  ⟶  𝒞(π₁(S, a)),    X ↦ F_a(X),
    ```

    carrying a finite étale covering of `S` to its geometric fibre with the natural left action of `π₁(S, a)`.

This equivalence translates the operations on coverings — finite products, disjoint unions, fibre products, quotients —
into the analogous operations on finite continuous `π₁(S, a)`-sets. In particular: an étale cover `X / S` is
**connected** (as an `𝒞 (S)`-object, hence as a topological space, by the discussion below) iff `π₁(S, a)` acts
transitively on `F_a(X)`.

### Connectedness of étale covers

A subtle point: connectedness in `𝒞(S)` (the categorical sense of file 29) might _a priori_ differ from topological
connectedness of `X`. They agree:

> An object `X ∈ 𝒞(S)` is connected in `𝒞(S)` iff it is topologically connected.

The argument: topological connected components of a finite étale cover of `S` are themselves finite étale covers of `S`
(since étale and finite are local properties, stable under restriction to a clopen subset). So a non-trivial topological
disconnection of `X` gives a categorical disconnection in `𝒞(S)`, and conversely.

By V.5.3, this means `π₁(S, a)` acts transitively on `F_a(X)` iff `X` is topologically connected.

### Faithful flatness and principal coverings

For an object `X ∈ 𝒞(S)` to be **faithfully flat and quasi- compact** over `S`, since it is already (finite, hence
quasi- compact) flat, it suffices that `X → S` is _surjective_, i.e. `X` is an epimorphism in `𝒞(S)`, equivalently
`X ≄ ∅_𝒞`. (Empty étale covers are excluded; non-empty connected ones are all finite faithfully flat surjective onto
`S`.)

By V.2.6 (iii), `X` is a **principal covering of `S` with group `G`** iff it is a principal homogeneous `G`-space in the
category `𝒞(S)`, in the V.5 sense. In `π₁(S, a)`-set terms: principal coverings correspond to _normal_ open subgroups
`N ⊆ π₁(S, a)` (with `G = π₁(S, a) / N`).

### Change of base point

Let `a, a′` be two geometric points of `S`. They define fibre functors `F_a, F_{a′}` on `𝒞(S)`, both fundamental, hence
isomorphic (V.5.7). The set of isomorphisms

```text
π₁(S; a, a′)  :=  Isom(F_a, F_{a′})
```

is the **set of path classes from `a` to `a′`** — the algebraic analogue of paths in topology, but considered up to
homotopy.

The collection of geometric points of `S` together with these path-class sets forms a **groupoid** (with composition the
pointwise composition of natural isomorphisms): the **fundamental groupoid of `S`**.

In particular:

- `π₁(S; a, a) = Aut(F_a) = π₁(S, a)`.
- For any two `a, a′`, `π₁(S; a, a′) ≠ ∅` (V.5.7 connectivity).
- A choice of path class `α ∈ π₁(S; a, a′)` defines an iso `α_* : π₁(S, a) → π₁(S, a′)`, canonical up to inner
    automorphism.

### The fundamental pro-group `Π₁^S`

By V.5.10, there is a canonical pro-group `Π₁^S` of `𝒞(S)`, the **fundamental pro-group of `S`** (or **local system of
fundamental groups on `S`**), characterised up to unique isomorphism by the functorial isomorphism

```text
F_a(Π₁^S)  ≃  π₁(S, a)
```

for every geometric point `a` of `S`.

If `s ∈ S` is an ordinary (= scheme-theoretic) point, the fibre of `Π₁^S` over `s` is a pro-group over `κ(s)`, a
projective limit of finite étale group schemes over `κ(s)`. We may call this pro-group the **fundamental group of `S` at
the ordinary point `s`** and denote it `π₁(S, s)`. By definition, its `Ω`- points (for `Ω` an algebraically closed
extension of `κ(s)`) are the elements of `π₁(S; a)` for the geometric point `a` defined by `Ω`.

In the special case `S = Spec(k)`: this construction associates to every field `k` a canonical pro-group over `k`,

```text
π₁(k)  :=  Π₁^{Spec(k)},
```

a projective limit of finite étale `k`-group schemes whose `Ω`-points (for `Ω / k` algebraically closed) form the
topological Galois group `Gal(k̄ / k)` (where `k̄ ⊂ Ω` is the separable closure of `k`); cf. V.8.1 below.

(Grothendieck's quiet remark: "this group `π₁(k)` does not seem yet to have attracted the attention of algebraists." The
modern reader knows it as the **Tannakian/anabelian gadget** encoded by the ground field.)

### Functoriality

Let

```text
f : S′  ⟶  S
```

be a morphism between two connected locally noetherian preschemes, let `a′` be a geometric point of `S′`, and let
`a := f ∘ a′` be its image in `S`. The base-change functor

```text
f^* : 𝒞(S)  ⟶  𝒞(S′),    X ↦ X ×_S S′
```

is well-defined (finite étale base-changes to finite étale). Moreover

```text
F_a  ≃  F_{a′} ∘ f^*,
```

so `f^*` carries a fibre functor on `𝒞(S)` to one on `𝒞(S′)`. By V.6.1 (file 32), `f^*` is **exact**. The associated
homomorphism (V.6.2)

```text
u  =  π₁(f; a′) : π₁(S′, a′)  ⟶  π₁(S, a)
```

is the **functoriality of `π₁`**. For composable maps `f, g`, `π₁(g f) = π₁(g) ∘ π₁(f)` (the contravariant
`^t`-formality of file 32 becomes covariant on `π₁` because `π = Aut(F)^{op}` flips the order).

For variable `a′` (and hence variable `a = f(a′)`), we get a homomorphism of pro-groups over `S′`,

```text
Π₁(f) : Π₁^{S′}  ⟶  f^* (Π₁^S),
```

with `f^*` the inverse-image of the pro-group structure.

If `S′` is itself an étale covering of `S` (i.e. `f : S′ → S` is finite étale), then `u : π₁(S′, a′) → π₁(S, a)` is an
**isomorphism onto an open subgroup**: namely, the stabiliser of `a′ ∈ F_a(S′)` in `π₁(S, a)`. (V.6.13 specialised.)
This is the algebraic analogue of "`π₁(B̃ / H, b̃) = H ⊆ π₁(B, b)`" for a connected covering of a topological space.

## §V.8 The case of a normal base prescheme

<!-- source: V.8 -->

### V.8.1 — `π₁(Spec k) = Gal(k̄ / k)`

<!-- source: V.8.1 -->

_This proposition says: the étale fundamental group of `Spec(k)` is the absolute Galois group of `k`._

**Proposition.** Let `S = Spec(k)`, and let `Ω` be an algebraically closed extension of `k`, defining a geometric point
`a` of `S` with values in `Ω`. Let `k̄` be the separable closure of `k` in `Ω`. There is a canonical isomorphism of
profinite groups

```text
π₁(Spec(k), a)  ≃  Gal(k̄ / k).
```

**Proof.** Let `k′` be the algebraic closure of `k` in `Ω`, defining a geometric point `b` of `S` with values in `k′`.
The natural map of fibre functors `F_b → F_a` is an iso: any `k`- homomorphism from a finite separable extension `L / k`
into `Ω` factors through `k̄` (since the image of `L` is contained in the algebraic closure of `k` in `Ω`, which is `k′`
containing `k̄`). Hence `π₁(S, a) ≃ π₁(S, b)`.

The group `π′ := Aut_k(k′ / k)` of `k`-automorphisms of `k′` acts on `F_b`; this defines a homomorphism
`π′ → Aut(F_b) ≃ Aut(F_a) = π₁(S, a)`. Now `π′` is canonically the absolute Galois group `Gal(k̄ / k)` (passing to
inverse limit of `Gal(L / k)` for finite Galois `L / k ⊂ k̄ ⊂ k′`, both yielding the same limit).

Verify the homomorphism is bijective:

- **Injective**: an element of the kernel acts trivially on every `F_b(Spec(L))` for`L/k` finite separable; this means
    it induces the identity on every finite separable subextension, hence is trivial.
- **Surjective**: a connected étale covering of `Spec(k)` is `Spec(L)` for `L / k` finite separable;`π'` acts
    transitively on the set of `k`-homomorphisms `L → k'` (since`L = k(α)` for some primitive element, and the
    conjugates of `α` are permuted by `π'`).

Hence `π′ ≅ π₁(S, a)`, completing the identification.

This **closes** the file 05 sidebar's forward-reference to the absolute Galois group, and the file 06 closing note's
forward-reference to the étale fundamental group of `Spec(k)`.

### V.8.2 — Normal base: `π₁(η) ↠ π₁(S)`

<!-- source: V.8.2 -->

_This proposition says: for a connected, locally noetherian, **normal** prescheme `S` with function field `K`, the
homomorphism `π₁(Spec K, ā) → π₁(S, a)` is **surjective**, and its kernel corresponds, via Galois theory, to the
subextension of `K̄ / K` composed of finite extensions of `K` in `Ω` that are **unramified over `S`**._

**Proposition.** Let `S` be a connected, locally noetherian, **normal** prescheme. Let `K = κ(s)` be its function field
(residue field at the generic point `s`). Let `Ω` be an algebraically closed extension of `K`, defining a geometric
point `a′` of `S′ := Spec(K)` and a geometric point `a` of `S`. Then the homomorphism

```text
π₁(S′, a′)  ⟶  π₁(S, a)
```

is **surjective**. Identifying the source with `Gal(K̄ / K)` via V.8.1, the kernel corresponds (by Galois theory) to the
subextension of `K̄ / K` composed of finite extensions of `K` in `Ω` that are **unramified over `S`** (i.e. those
`L ⊂ K̄` for which the integral closure of `𝒪_S` in `L` defines an étale cover of `S`).

**Proof.** Surjectivity is the assertion: the inverse image on `S′` of a connected étale covering `X → S` is connected.
This is exactly the I.10.1 (file 12) result, which says: an étale cover of a connected normal scheme is integral over
its function field.

The kernel description: an automorphism `τ ∈ Gal(K̄ / K)` lies in the kernel iff for every connected étale cover
`X / S`, `τ` acts trivially on `F_a(X)`. Since `F_a(X)` is in bijection with the `K`-embeddings of the
function-field-of-`X` into `Ω`, and these embeddings are exactly the embeddings of the finite *unramified-over-`S`*
subextensions of `K̄`, the kernel is the absolute Galois group of the **maximal unramified extension** of
`K`in`K̄`relative to`S`.

**Remark.** This identification of `π₁(S, a)` with the Galois group of the maximal unramified extension was, in the case
of normal arithmetic schemes, the "classical" definition of the fundamental group long before Grothendieck — but only
for normal `S`. The point of Grothendieck's general construction is to extend the definition uniformly to all (locally
noetherian, connected) `S`, normal or not.

## §V.9 The case of nonconnected preschemes; multi-Galois categories

<!-- source: V.9 -->

If `S` is locally noetherian but **not** connected, with connected components `(S_i)_{i ∈ I}`, the category of finite
étale coverings of `S` decomposes as a product:

```text
𝒞(S)  ≃  ∏_{i ∈ I}  𝒞(S_i),
```

with each factor a Galois category in the sense of §5. After choosing one geometric point `a_i` per `S_i`, the
description of `𝒞(S_i)` in terms of `π₁(S_i, a_i)` gives a presentation of `𝒞(S)` as a product of `𝒞(π_i)`'s.

For descent applications, choosing a base point in every connected component is sometimes inconvenient. The cleaner
formulation: extend V.5.8 from a single Galois category to a **product of Galois categories** indexed by a groupoid, by
treating the **groupoid of geometric points of `S`** (an arbitrary groupoid `S` of geometric points, with each point
hitting at least one connected component) as the indexing object.

A category equivalent to a product of categories of the form `𝒞(π)` is called a **multi-Galois category**. We leave the
combinatorial details to the reader (per Grothendieck's
"`one may call ... multi-Galois categories. We leave the details to the reader.`").

## Worked examples

### `S = Spec 𝔽_p`

By V.8.1, `π₁(Spec 𝔽_p, ā) = Gal(𝔽̄_p / 𝔽_p)`. The Galois group of`𝔽̄_p / 𝔽_p` is **topologically generated by
Frobenius**

```text
Frob_p :  x ↦ x^p,
```

with `Frob_p` acting on `𝔽_{pⁿ}` (the unique degree-`n` extension of `𝔽_p` in `𝔽̄_p`) as the generator of the cyclic
group `Gal(𝔽_{pⁿ} / 𝔽_p) = ℤ / nℤ`. Passing to the limit:

```text
π₁(Spec 𝔽_p, ā)  =  Gal(𝔽̄_p / 𝔽_p)  =  lim_n ℤ / nℤ  =  ℤ̂,
```

with `ℤ̂` the topological closure of `ℤ ⋅ Frob_p` in the profinite topology.

The equivalence `𝒞(Spec 𝔽_p) ≃ 𝒞(ℤ̂)` of file 31 reads off concretely: a connected étale cover of `Spec 𝔽_p` is
`Spec 𝔽_{pⁿ}` for some `n ≥ 1`, with `ℤ̂` acting on its geometric fibre (= the `n`-element set of `𝔽̄_p`-points
of`Spec 𝔽_{pⁿ}`) through its cyclic quotient `ℤ̂ → ℤ / nℤ` and the cyclic action on `n` elements.

This **closes** the file 05 sidebar's foreshadowing (`Frobenius generates Ẑ for k = 𝔽_q`).

### `S = Spec ℤ`

By a deep theorem of **Minkowski** (the discriminant inequality of _Geometrie der Zahlen_; see
[`minkowski-discriminant`](appendix-black-boxes.md)), the discriminant of any non-trivial number field `K ≠ ℚ` is
strictly larger than `1` in absolute value. Geometrically: the ring of integers `𝒪_K` of any non-trivial finite
extension `K / ℚ` is **ramified** at some prime `p`, i.e. `Spec(𝒪_K) → Spec ℤ` fails to be étale at some prime. Hence

```text
π₁(Spec ℤ, η̄)  =  (1).
```

(Equivalently: there are no non-trivial étale coverings of `Spec ℤ`. The "tower of integers" admits no unramified
non-trivial branchings.)

This is a striking consequence of arithmetic: the absolute analogue of "`Spec ℤ` is simply connected" holds for the
étale fundamental group, in stark contrast to topology where `ℝ` and similar spaces have rich fundamental groups.

### `S = Spec ℤ[1/2]`

The cover

```text
Spec ℤ[√2]  ⟶  Spec ℤ[1/2]
```

is finite étale (file 30 worked example: trivial inertia at all odd primes, and `2` is inverted), of degree `2`. It is a
**principal covering** with Galois group `ℤ / 2`. By the equivalence `𝒞(Spec ℤ[1/2]) ≃ 𝒞(π₁(Spec ℤ[1/2], η̄))`, this
corresponds to an open subgroup `U ⊂ π₁(Spec ℤ[1/2], η̄)` of index `2`, with quotient `π₁ / U = ℤ / 2` acting on the
geometric fibre.

Since `ℤ[1/2] ⊂ ℚ` is normal, V.8.2 says

```text
π₁(Spec ℤ[1/2], η̄)  =  Gal(M / ℚ),
```

where `M` is the **maximal subextension of`ℚ̄ / ℚ` unramified outside 2** (i.e. the compositum of all finite extensions
of `ℚ` unramified at every odd prime). The fact that `Spec ℤ[√2]` provides an open subgroup of index`2` matches:
`ℚ(√2) / ℚ` is one such finite unramified-outside-`2` extension.

Splitting behaviour computed in file 06 (where odd primes split or are inert in `ℤ[√2]`) is now placed in its
`π₁`-context: the action of`Gal(M / ℚ)` on the `ℤ / 2`-set `{e, σ}`is the sign of the Galois group of`ℚ(√2) / ℚ`,
restricted from the maximal unramified-outside-`2` extension.

### `ℰ =` elliptic curve over good-reduction`Spec ℤ[1/Δ]`

Take an elliptic curve `ℰ : y² = x³ + ax + b` over`Spec ℤ` with discriminant `Δ = 4a³ + 27b² ∈ ℤ ∖ {0}`. Restricted to
`S := Spec ℤ[1/Δ]`, `ℰ → S` is **smooth proper**, and we may consider `π₁^{ét}(ℰ_S, ē)` for a geometric point `ē` of
`ℰ_S`.

Comparison with topology: pulling back to `ℂ` gives a complex elliptic curve`ℰ*ℂ`, topologically a real torus `S¹ × S¹`.
The topological fundamental group is `π₁^top(ℰ*ℂ, ē) = ℤ²`. By the **comparison theorem** (file 28 analogy +
Grothendieck's GAGA-style transcendence theorem, Exposé XII of SGA 1 — out of scope for the gentle reader),

```text
π₁^{ét}(ℰ_ℂ, ē)  ≃  π̂₁^top(ℰ_ℂ, ē)  =  ℤ̂².
```

The "algebraic = profinite of topological" slogan from file 28 holds here verbatim. (For the _algebraic_ fundamental
group `π₁^{ét}(ℰ_S, ē)` over`S = Spec ℤ[1/Δ]` — i.e. without extending scalars to `ℂ` — the structure is more intricate,
involving the Galois action on `ℤ̂²` that encodes the arithmetic of`ℰ` over the rational primes; this is the content of
the **specialisation theorem** SGA 1 X.)

## Closing the project

The arc that opened with the étale theory of Exposé I, continued with the smooth theory of Exposé II, the lifting theory
of Exposé III, opened the flatness boxes with Exposé IV, and now culminates in the étale fundamental group of Exposé V
ties together cleanly. **Five slogans in five exposés**:

- **Étale = smooth + relative dimension `0`.** (Exposé I + II) The four rigidity stories of file 11 — open immersion,
    lifting, complete-local-ring classification, local form `(A[t] / F)_{(F'(t))}` — are the relative-dimension-`0`
    content of the file-15 differential machinery. Where smoothness gives `Ω¹` locally free of rank `dim_x f`, étaleness
    forces `dim_x f = 0` and so `Ω¹ = 0`, recovering file 04's definition of unramified.

- **Smooth = locally étale over a polynomial scheme.** (Exposé II) The Jacobian criterion II.4.10 (file 15) reads
    classical: for a hypersurface, smoothness at a point is `dF(x₀) ≠ 0` — the test any working geometer uses. The
    differential exact sequence II.4.3 splits for smooth morphisms.

- **Formally smooth = lifting along nilpotent thickenings; smoothness + cohomological vanishing = global lifting.**
    (Exposé III) The lifting characterisation (III.2.1, file 19) makes formal smoothness portable to formal and analytic
    settings. The H¹/H² obstruction theory (file 21) puts deformation theory on a precise cohomological footing. The
    algebraisation theorem (file 22, III.7.3) and its curve corollary (III.7.4) deliver the headline arithmetic
    application: **every smooth proper curve lifts from characteristic `p` to characteristic `0`**.

- **Flat = exactness of `M ⊗_A −`; faithfully flat = exactness with reflection of exactness; the flat locus is open;
    flat morphisms are open.** (Exposé IV) The local flatness criterion (IV.5.6, file 26) makes verifications in
    arithmetic and deformation-theoretic settings tractable: check on the special fibre plus Tor-vanishing, conclude on
    the whole base. The open-image theorem (IV.6.6) and openness of the flat locus (IV.6.10) make flatness a
    Zariski-local property of the source. **Generic flatness** (IV.6.11) is the spread-out theorem used freely in
    arithmetic geometry: deformations from generic-fibre data to neighbouring fibres exist over a Zariski open. The
    black boxes that have been carrying water since file 04 are now opened in full.

- **The étale fundamental group `π₁(S, a)` is the Galois group of the universal finite étale cover of `S`. Equivalently,
    the category of finite étale coverings of `S` is equivalent to the category of finite continuous `π₁(S, a)`-sets.**
    (Exposé V) The classification (V.4.1, file 31) gives a uniform description of the étale-cover category: pick a
    geometric point, get a profinite group; the equivalence `𝒞(S) ≃ 𝒞(π₁(S, a))` translates every covering operation
    into a `π₁`-set operation. The functoriality V.6 (file 32) makes `π₁` into a covariant functor on connected pointed
    schemes, and the case-of-a-field V.8.1 (`π₁(Spec k) = Gal(k̄/k)`) / case-of-a-normal-base V.8.2 (`π₁(η) ↠   π₁(S)`
    with ramification kernel) recover classical Galois theory and **algebraic class field theory** as special cases.

Toy-example arc closure (one example per exposé, threading the arithmetic story):

- **`Spec ℤ[√2]`** — étale on `Spec ℤ[1/2]`, ramified at `2` (file 04 introduced; file 30 computed inertia; file 33 sees
    `π₁(Spec ℤ[1/2])` has it as an open subgroup of index`2`).
- **`Spec ℤ[t]`** — flat over `Spec ℤ`; smooth on the open of good reduction; the simplest smooth `Spec ℤ`-scheme.
- **The elliptic curve `ℰ : y² = x³ + ax + b` over `Spec ℤ`** — flat everywhere (IV); smooth on `Spec ℤ[1/Δ]` (II);
    lifts smoothly to characteristic `0` (III); has `π₁^ét(ℰ_ℂ) = ℤ̂²` over`ℂ` (V).
- **`Spec 𝔽_p`** — `π₁ = ℤ̂`, generated by Frobenius. The analytic-geometric prototype: étale theory on a single closed
    point.
- **`Spec ℤ`** — `π₁ = (1)` by Minkowski's discriminant inequality. The arithmetic Schwarz lemma for the integers.

What remains beyond the gentle reading:

- **The specialisation theorem** of Exposé X: as the geometric point varies along a connected base, the geometric `π₁`
    of the fibre is, after taking the prime-to-`p` part, invariant under specialisation. The arithmetic upshot: for a
    smooth proper curve `C / S` over a connected `S`, the prime-to-`p` part of `π₁^ét(C_{s̄})` is independent of the
    geometric point `s̄`.
- **Faithfully flat descent** (Exposés VIII and IX) — the geometric specialisation of the abstract descent isomorphism
    of file 38 (VI.12.1) to the case of faithfully flat covers of schemes. The framework — fibred categories, cleavages,
    pseudofunctors, the descent isomorphism — is now developed in files 34–38 (Exposé VI). Exposé VIII gives descent of
    quasi-coherent sheaves and morphisms; Exposé IX gives descent of finite étale morphisms (the rigorous form of what
    files 30 V.2.6, 31 V.3.6, and 33 V.7 cited as [`bourbaki-sem-190`](appendix-black-boxes.md#bourbaki-sem-190)).
- **GAGA** (Exposé XII) — the comparison theorem `π₁^{ét} = π₁^{top̂}` for smooth proper varieties over`ℂ`.

The reading-guide pointer (file 00) names Lenstra and Cadoret/Murre as modern references for the rest of the story:
specifically the comparison theorem (Murre §6.2, Lenstra §6), specialisation (Murre §9), and purity (Murre §10).

The étale, smooth, formally smooth, flat, and Galois theories are now all in hand. The toy examples — `ℤ[√2]` for étale,
`ℤ[t]` and the elliptic curve for smooth, lifts of `𝔽_p`-curves to `ℤ_p`-curves for formally smooth, the same elliptic
curve over `Spec ℤ` for flat, `Spec 𝔽_p` and`Spec ℤ` for the fundamental group — sit inside the same arithmetic universe
and exhibit the regimes (relative dimension `0` / positive, finite-type / formal, smooth / flat, classical / Galois) of
one underlying theory.

A reader who has finished file 33 has read a coherent companion to the étale arc of the first five exposés of SGA 1. The
original source remains in the source-translation files at `docs/books/sga/i/`; the gentle rewrite is in this directory;
the glossary, notation, and black-box appendix files close the project from the index side.
