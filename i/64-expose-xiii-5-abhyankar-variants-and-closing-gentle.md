# Expose XIII §5 (gentle) — Abhyankar variants and the gentle SGA closing

<!-- source: XIII.5 -->

## What this file is about

Abhyankar's lemma is a recipe for untangling a controlled cover. Given a cover that spreads out along a thin locus of
forbidden points, one can adjoin roots to the base and obtain a new cover that is clean — unramified everywhere —
over the new base. The recipe works only for the controlled flavour of spreading-out, the tame one. The opposite
flavour, wild, is the bad ramification that the prime characteristic of the base field forces, and no construction
straightens it out. An earlier file proved a local form. The geometric setting now needs a family version, with the
roots adjoined simultaneously in every direction.

The relative lemma also closes the thirteen-chapter tour. The gentle SGA project read Grothendieck's first seminar in
a voice accessible to a working algebraic geometer rather than to a specialist. The closing remarks point forward to
the later seminars.

File 51 (X.3) proved Abhyankar's lemma in the local form that file 50 (X.2.6) needs. File 62 (XIII.2.4) needed a sharper
*relative* version: an étale covering of `U` tamely ramified along an NC divisor `D` extends, after a Kummer base
change, to an étale covering of all of `X`. This is the **absolute Abhyankar lemma** XIII.5.2, with relative refinements
XIII.5.3–XIII.5.6. File 64 reproduces all the variants in the gentle voice and uses them to close the gentle SGA
project.

The structure of the file is: XIII.5.1 (the local Kummer cover), XIII.5.2 (the absolute Abhyankar lemma), XIII.5.3,
XIII.5.3.0 (the strictly-local picture and the resulting tame fundamental-group calculation), XIII.5.4 (the smoothness
statement of the relative Kummer model), XIII.5.5 (the relative Abhyankar lemma proper), XIII.5.6 (the universal tame
covering of a strict-local NC neighbourhood). The closing section walks through the thirteen-exposé gentle SGA tour and
points forward to SGA 4 / SGA 4½ / SGA 5.

## §XIII.5.1 — Local Kummer at a regular NC point

<!-- source: XIII.5.1 -->

*This section says:* if `X = Spec A` is a regular local scheme with `D = ∑ div(f_i)` cut out by part of a regular system
of parameters, and `n_i` are integers prime to the residual characteristic `p`, then the explicit Kummer cover
`X' = X[T_1, …, T_r] / (T_i^{n_i} − f_i)` is regular, the boundary `∑ div(T_i)` is again NC, and `U' = U ×_X X'` is a
connected tame étale cover of `U` relative to `D`.

### XIII.5.1 — Proposition.

<!-- source: XIII.5.1 -->

Let `X = Spec A` be a regular local scheme, and let

```text
D = ∑_{1 ≤ i ≤ r} div(f_i)
```

be an NC divisor where the `f_i ∈ 𝔪_A` are part of a regular system of parameters of `A`. Let `n_i ≥ 0` be integers and
put

```text
X' = X[T_1, …, T_r] / (T_1^{n_1} − f_1, …, T_r^{n_r} − f_r),
U' = U ×_X X'.
```

Then `X'` is regular, and `U'` is the complement in `X'` of the NC divisor `∑_{1 ≤ i ≤ r} div(T_i)`. If the `n_i` are
prime to the residual characteristic `p` of `X`, then `U' → U` is a connected étale cover of `U`, tamely ramified
relative to `D` (XIII.2.3 c).

*Proof.* `X'` is the spectrum of a local ring `A'` whose maximal ideal is generated by `T_1, …, T_r`. We may suppose
`r = dim(A)`. Since `A'` is finite and flat over `A` (it is a finite product of monomial extensions, and Kummer
extensions of a regular local ring by uniformiser radicals stay finite flat), its dimension is again `r`. Hence `A'` is
regular (EGA `0_IV` 17.1.1), and the `T_i` form a regular system of parameters of `A'`.

If the `n_i` are prime to `p`, every `f_i` is invertible on `U`, so the equations `T_i^{n_i} = f_i` define an étale
extension on `U` by I.7.4 (file 11). The étale cover `U' → U` is tame relative to `D`: at the generic point `x_i` of
`V(f_i)` and its strict localisation `R̄ = (𝒪_{X, x_i})^{sh}` with fraction field `K̄`, the `K̄`-algebra representing
`U'|_{K̄}` is obtained from the field `K̄[T_i] / (T_i^{n_i} − f_i)` by making an unramified extension; it is therefore
tame over `R̄` (the inertia at `T_i` is cyclic of order `n_i`, prime to `p`).

Connectedness of `U'` follows from the irreducibility of `T_i^{n_i} − f_i` over the fraction field of the regular local
ring `A` localised at `f_i`, when `n_i` is prime to `p` (a standard Kummer-theoretic fact).

Slogan: **a tame Kummer extension along the components of an NC divisor stays inside the étale category on the
complement; conversely (XIII.5.2), every tame étale cover *is* a Kummer extension.**

## §XIII.5.2 — The absolute Abhyankar lemma

<!-- source: XIII.5.2 -->

### XIII.5.2 — Proposition.

<!-- source: XIII.5.2 -->

Let `X` be a regular local scheme, `D = ∑_{1 ≤ i ≤ r} div(f_i)` an NC divisor with the `f_i` part of a regular system of
parameters (as in XIII.5.1). Let `Y = Supp D`, `U = X − Y`, and let `V → U` be an étale cover tamely ramified relative
to `D`. For each `i`, let `x_i` be the generic point of `V(f_i)`; then `𝒪_{X, x_i}` is a DVR with fraction field `K_i`,
and

```text
V|_{K_i}  =  Spec( ∏_{j ∈ J_i} L_j ),
```

where the `L_j` are finite separable extensions of `K_i`. Let `n_j` denote the order of the inertia group of a Galois
extension generated by `L_j`, and let `n_i` be the lcm of the `n_j` as `j` ranges over `J_i`. With

```text
X' = X[T_1, …, T_r] / (T_1^{n_1} − f_1, …, T_r^{n_r} − f_r),
U' = U ×_X X',  V' = V ×_X X',
```

the étale cover `V' → U'` extends uniquely, up to unique isomorphism, to an étale cover of `X'`, and the `n_i` are prime
to the residual characteristic `p` of `X`.

*Slogan.* **Every tame étale cover of `U` becomes unramified after the Kummer base change `X' → X` determined by the
inertia exponents at the boundary components.**

### Proof of XIII.5.2.

#### Uniqueness.

Since `X'` is normal (XIII.5.1), an étale cover of `X'` extending `V'` is isomorphic to the normalisation of `X'` in the
fibre of `V'` at the generic point of `X'` (I.10.2 of file 12). So uniqueness up to unique iso is automatic.

#### Existence: étale-local reduction.

Let `x̄'` be a geometric point of `Y' = V(T_1) ∪ … ∪ V(T_r) ⊆ X'`. Write `X̄'` for the strict localisation of `X'` at
`x̄'`, `V̄' = V' ×_{X'} X̄'`, etc. By descent and uniqueness, it suffices to show that for every such `x̄'`, the étale
cover `V̄'` of `Ū'` extends to an étale cover of `X̄'`.

#### Extension across codim `≥ 2`.

By [`sga-2-xiv-1-11`](appendix-black-boxes.md#sga-2-xiv-1-11) (SGA 2 XIV 1.11), an étale cover of an open subset of a
regular scheme `X̄'` that contains every point of codimension `≤ 1` extends uniquely to all of `X̄'`. So we may restrict
to geometric points `x̄'` *projecting to a maximal point of `Y'`* — i.e. to the generic point of a single irreducible
component `V(T_{i_0})`.

#### Extension at a maximal point.

At a geometric point `x̄'` above a maximal point of `Y'`, `X̄'_{red}` is the spectrum of a DVR (the local ring at the
generic point of `V(T_{i_0})` after strict henselisation). The étale cover `V̄'` of `Ū'` is tame at that DVR (since `V`
was tame at `X_{x_{i_0}}`, and we have absorbed the inertia exponent `n_{i_0}` into the Kummer base change). At a
strictly-local DVR, an étale cover of the punctured spectrum that is tame at the missing point extends to the whole
spectrum — this is file 51's X.3.6.

This completes the construction of the extension.

#### The `n_i` are prime to `p`.

Suppose for contradiction `p | n_{i_0}` for some `i_0`. After replacing `X` by

```text
X[T_{i_0}, T_i]_{i ≠ i_0} / (T_{i_0}^{n_{i_0} / p} − f_{i_0}, T_i^{n_i} − f_i),
```

we are reduced to the case `X' = X[T] / (T^p − f_{i_0})`. The argument above gives an extension `W'` of `V'` to `X'`
(over `X` we may suppose strictly local). Let `Z = V(p) ⊆ X` and `Z_1 = Z ∩ X_{f_1}`; `Z_1` is a non-empty open of `Z`.
The étale cover `V'` of `U'` extends to an étale cover `W'` of `X'` (already established). Let `W''_1, W''_2` be the
inverse images of `W'` by the two projections `X'' = X' ×_X X' ⇉ X'`, and consider the descent isomorphism
`u : W''_1|_{U''} → W''_2|_{U''}`. We must show `u` extends to an `X''`-morphism `W''_1 → W''_2`, which would then be
descent data on `W'` for `X' → X` and would force `V` to extend to `X`.

Let `Z'' = X'' ×_X Z`, `Z''_1 = X'' ×_X Z_1`. The morphism `Z'' → Z` is *radicial* (because `X'' → X` is, modulo `p`),
so there is an isomorphism `v : W''_1|_{Z''} → W''_2|_{Z''}` extending `u|_{Z''_1}`. Since `X` is henselian,
`Hom_{X''}(W''_1, W''_2) ≃ Hom_{Z''}(W''_1|_{Z''}, W''_2|_{Z''})`, so `v` lifts to a morphism `w : W''_1 → W''_2`. The
subscheme of `X''` on which `u` and `w` agree is both open and closed (a closed-and-open subset of `X''`), and it
contains `Z''_1`, hence is all of `X''`. This shows `V` extends to `X`, hence `n_{i_0} = 1`, contradicting
`p | n_{i_0}`.

This completes the proof.

Slogan: **the absolute Abhyankar lemma is the codim-2 extension theorem of SGA 2 XIV plus X.3.6 plus a henselian-
descent step; the prime-to-`p` conclusion comes from a clean contradiction at the radicial closed fibre.**

## §XIII.5.3, XIII.5.3.0 — Strictly-local picture and `π_1^tame` of a strict-local NC neighbourhood

<!-- source: XIII.5.3 -->

### XIII.5.3.0 — Tame coverings of a strictly-local NC neighbourhood.

<!-- source: XIII.5.3.0 -->

Resume the hypotheses and notation of XIII.5.2, and assume moreover that `X` is *strictly local*. Then every connected
tame étale cover of `U` relative to `D` is a quotient of a Kummer cover of the form

```text
U' = U[T_1, …, T_r] / (T_1^{n_1} − f_1, …, T_r^{n_r} − f_r),
```

with the `n_i` prime to `p`. Let `μ_n ⊆ X` be the group of `n`-th roots of unity (a finite étale `X`-group scheme, since
`X` is strictly local). The group of `U`-automorphisms of `U'` is then

```text
μ_{n_1} × μ_{n_2} × … × μ_{n_r},
```

an `n_i`-th root of unity `ξ_i` acting on `U'` by `T_i ↦ ξ_i T_i`.

### XIII.5.3 — Corollary (`π_1^tame` of a strict-local NC neighbourhood).

<!-- source: XIII.5.3 -->

Let `X` be a strictly local regular scheme of residual characteristic `p ≥ 0`, `D = ∑_{1 ≤ i ≤ r} div(f_i)` an NC
divisor, `U = X − Supp D`. Define

```text
Ũ  :=  lim_{(n_i)}  U[T_1, …, T_r] / (T_1^{n_1} − f_1, …, T_r^{n_r} − f_r),
```

the projective limit over the filtered ordered set (under divisibility) of tuples `(n_i)` of positive integers prime to
`p`. Then `Ũ` is a **universal tame étale cover of `U`** relative to `D`, and

```text
π_1^tame(U)  ≃  ∏_{ℓ ≠ p}  ℤ_ℓ(1)^r       (canonical iso),
```

where `ℤ_ℓ(1) = lim_{n > 0} μ_{ℓ^n}` (with the limit taken over powers of `ℓ` prime to `p`). Non-canonically,
`π_1^tame(U) ≃ (Π_{ℓ ≠ p} ℤ_ℓ)^r`.

*Proof.* By XIII.5.3.0 every connected tame étale cover of `U` is a quotient of a finite-Kummer cover
`U[T_i] / (T_i^{n_i} − f_i)`; passing to the limit identifies `Ũ` with the universal tame cover. The `U`-automorphism
calculation in XIII.5.3.0 identifies `Aut_U(Ũ) = ∏ lim_n μ_n = ∏_{ℓ ≠ p} ℤ_ℓ(1)`, and there are `r` factors (one per
component of the NC divisor).

The non-canonical iso follows by choosing a uniformiser-by-uniformiser identification `ℤ_ℓ(1) ≃ ℤ_ℓ` (depends on a
choice of primitive roots of unity).

Slogan for XIII.5.3: **strict-locally at an NC point, the tame fundamental group is `Π_{ℓ ≠ p} ℤ_ℓ(1)^r`, one factor per
boundary component, generated by the Kummer-cover monodromy.**

## §XIII.5.4 — Smoothness of the relative Kummer model

<!-- source: XIII.5.4 -->

### XIII.5.4 — Proposition.

<!-- source: XIII.5.4 -->

Let `f : X → S` be a morphism, `D = ∑_{1 ≤ i ≤ r} div(f_i)` an NC divisor relative to `S` (XIII.5.4's hypothesis is the
strict-NC version of XIII.2.1.0). For a point `x` of `Y = Supp D` let `I(x) = { i : f_i(x) = 0 }`. Suppose
`V((f_i)_{i ∈ I(x)})` is smooth over `S` of codimension `#I(x)` in `X`. Let `U = X − Y`. Fix `x ∈ Y`, set
`X_1 = Spec 𝒪_{X, x}`, `U_1 = U ×_X X_1`, integers `(n_i)_{i ∈ I(x)}`, and

```text
X' = X[T_i]_{i ∈ I(x)} / (T_i^{n_i} − f_i).
```

Let `x' ∈ X'` be the unique point above `x`. Then `X'` is *smooth over `S` at `x'`*. If the `n_i` are prime to the
residual characteristic `p` of `κ(x)`, then `U'_1 = U_1 ×_X X'` is a *connected tame étale cover of `U_1`* relative to
`S_1`, where `S_1` is the image of `X_1` in `S` (XIII.2.1.1).

*Proof.* Set `s = f(x)`. The geometric fibre `X'_{s̄}` is regular at `x'` by XIII.5.1 (the absolute statement applied to
the regular local scheme `X_{s̄}`). `X'` is flat over `S` in a neighbourhood of `Y` (the Kummer model is a finite flat
cover; its flatness propagates from the absolute case via étale-flat base change). Regularity of the geometric fibre +
flatness ⇒ smoothness (EGA IV 12.1.6).

If the `n_i` are prime to `p`, étaleness of `U'_1 → U_1` is I.7.4 (file 11). The tameness is checked fibre by fibre
using XIII.5.1. Connectedness of `U'_1` follows from SGA 4 XVI 3.2
([`sga-4-xvi-base-change-smooth`](appendix-black-boxes.md#sga-4-xvi-base-change-smooth)).

## §XIII.5.5 — The relative Abhyankar lemma

<!-- source: XIII.5.5 -->

### XIII.5.5 — Proposition (relative Abhyankar).

<!-- source: XIII.5.5 -->

Let `X` be an `S`-scheme, `D = ∑_{1 ≤ i ≤ r} div(f_i)` an NC divisor relative to `S` as in XIII.5.4. Let `Y = Supp D`,
`U = X − Y`, `x ∈ Y`, `X_1 = X_{(x)}` the strict localisation at a geometric point above `x`, `U_1 = U ×_X X_1`, and let
`V_1` be an étale cover of `U_1`. Suppose that for every maximal point `s` of `S`, the restriction `V_1|_{s̄}` is tamely
ramified over `X_1|_{s̄}` relative to `s̄`. Then there exist integers `n_i` (`i ∈ I(x)`) prime to the residual
characteristic `p` of `κ(x)`, such that, with

```text
X'_1 = X_1 [T_i]_{i ∈ I(x)} / (T_i^{n_i} − f_i),
U'_1 = U_1 ×_{X_1} X'_1,
V'_1 = V_1 ×_{X_1} X'_1,
```

the étale cover `V'_1 → U'_1` extends *uniquely up to unique iso* to an étale cover of `X'_1`. In particular `V_1` is
tamely ramified over `X_1` relative to `S`.

*Slogan.* **The absolute Abhyankar lemma XIII.5.2 promoted to the relative `S`-setting: tameness over every maximal
point of `S` implies tameness over `S`, with a Kummer base change controlled by inertia at each boundary component.**

### Proof of XIII.5.5.

We may suppose `S` is local noetherian with closed point `f(x)`.

#### Choosing the `n_i`.

For each maximal point `s` of `S` and each `i ∈ I(x)`, let `x_{i, s}` be the generic point of the closed subset `V(f_i)`
of the fibre `X_1|_{s̄}`. The local ring `(𝒪_{X_1, x_{i, s}})_{red}` is a DVR with fraction field `K_{i, s}`, and
`V_1|_{K_{i, s}} = Spec(∏_{j ∈ J_{i, s}} L_j)` with `L_j` finite separable over `K_{i, s}`. Let `n_{j, s}` be the order
of the inertia group of a Galois closure of `L_j`; let `n_i` be the lcm of the `n_{j, s}` as `s` ranges over the maximal
points of `S` and `j` over `J_{i, s}`.

#### Uniqueness of the extension.

The smoothness of `X'_1` over `S` at the points of `Y` (XIII.5.4) gives `prof_ét(Y'_1, X'_1) ≥ 2` (SGA 4 XVI 3.2 or SGA
2 XIV 1.19). The étale-cover-extension theorem at depth `≥ 2` gives uniqueness.

#### Existence at maximal points of `Y'_1`.

Let `x̄'_1` be a geometric point of `Y'_1 = V(T_1) ∪ … ∪ V(T_r)` above a maximal point of `Y'_1`. Write `X̄'_1` for the
strict localisation at `x̄'_1`. Then `X̄'_1|_{red}` is the spectrum of a DVR (the local ring at the generic point of a
single component `V(T_{i_0})`). The étale cover `V̄'_1` of `Ū'_1` is tame at that DVR (by construction of `n_i`), so
`V̄'_1` extends to `X̄'_1` by file 51 X.3.6.

#### Extension across the remaining codimensions.

We have an extension over an open `W'_1 ⊆ X'_1` containing the maximal points of `Y'_1`. Set `Z'_1 = X'_1 − W'_1`. For
`s` a maximal point of `S`, `codim(Z'_1|_s, X'_1|_s) ≥ 2`; for `s` a non-maximal point, `codim(Z'_1|_s, X'_1|_s) ≥ 1`
and `prof_ét_s(S) ≥ 1`. By SGA 2 XIV 1.20, the extension across `Z'_1` is automatic.

#### The `n_i` are prime to `p`.

Same contradiction argument as in XIII.5.2: if `p | n_{i_0}`, replace `n_{i_0}` by `n_{i_0} / p` and argue that the
extension forces `n_{i_0} = 1`, contradicting `p | n_{i_0}`. The henselian-descent step uses radicial fibre + strict-
local henselianity, exactly as in XIII.5.2.

This completes the proof of XIII.5.5.

## §XIII.5.6 — Universal tame covering of a strict-local NC neighbourhood relative to `S`

<!-- source: XIII.5.6 -->

### XIII.5.6 — Corollary.

<!-- source: XIII.5.6 -->

Let `X` be an `S`-scheme, `D = ∑_{1 ≤ i ≤ r} div(f_i)` an NC divisor relative to `S` as in XIII.5.4. Let `x̄` be a
geometric point of `X`, `X̄` the strict localisation at `x̄`, `Ȳ = Y ×_X X̄`, `Ū = X̄ − Ȳ`, and

```text
Ũ  :=  lim_{(n_i)}  Ū [T_i]_{i ∈ I(x)} / (T_i^{n_i} − f_i),
```

the projective limit over `(n_i)` prime to the residual characteristic `p` of `κ(x)`. Then `Ũ` is a **universal tame
covering of `Ū` relative to `S`**, and

```text
π_1^tame(Ū)  ≃  ∏_{ℓ ≠ p}  ℤ_ℓ(1)^{I(x)}       (canonical iso).
```

Non-canonically, `π_1^tame(Ū) ≃ (∏_{ℓ ≠ p} ℤ_ℓ)^{I(x)}`.

*Proof.* XIII.5.5 says every connected tame étale cover of `Ū` arises by quotient from a Kummer cover; passing to the
limit gives the universal property and identifies the automorphism group as in XIII.5.3.

### XIII.5.6.1 — Stratification of `X` by intersections of boundary components.

<!-- source: XIII.5.6.1 -->

For every subset `I ⊆ [1, r]`, put

```text
X_I  =  (⋂_{i ∈ I} V(f_i))  ∩  (⋂_{i ∉ I} X_{f_i}).
```

The `X_I` form a stratification of `X` by locally closed subschemes; XIII.5.6 says `π_1^tame` at a stratum `X_I` is
`∏_{ℓ ≠ p} ℤ_ℓ(1)^I`, one `ℤ_ℓ(1)` factor per component of the boundary the point sees. This is the *local model* of the
tame fundamental group along an NC divisor in arbitrary dimension.

The gentle SGA pass through SGA 1 is complete with file 64. The thirteen exposés covered are:

- **Exposé I (étale morphisms).** Files 10–12 (with the preparation arc 01–06 and prep file 23).
- **Exposé II (smooth morphisms).** Files 14–16.
- **Exposé III (extension properties of smooth morphisms; lifting).** Files 19–22 (with prep files 17–18).
- **Exposé IV (flat morphisms).** Files 24–27.
- **Exposé V (the fundamental group: generalities).** Files 30–33 (with prep files 28–29).
- **Exposé VI (fibred categories and descent).** Files 35–38 (with prep file 34).
- **Exposé VIII (descent of quasi-coherent modules and morphisms).** Files 40–43.
- **Exposé IX (descent of étale morphisms; effectivity; Stein factorisation).** Files 44–47.
- **Exposé X (Specialisation of `π_1`; the transcendental method; Abhyankar).** Files 48–51.
- **Exposé XI (Projective spaces, abelian varieties, principal bundles, Kummer / Artin–Schreier).** Files 52–56.
- **Exposé XII (analytic geometry, GAGA, Riemann existence).** Files 57–60.
- **Exposé XIII (cohomological properness).** Files 61–64.

The single exposé of SGA 1 *not* given a gentle pass is **Exposé VII** (la descente fidèlement plate des revêtements).
The étale-fundamental-group story of Exposé V (file 33) and the cohomological-properness story of Exposés IX and XIII
together cover the material readers most often want from VII; SGA 1 VII itself is short and mostly soritic.

#### What we did *not* prove.

- **SGA 4 itself** — the full étale-cohomology framework. Files 61–64 use it as a standing bridge (file 61 §XIII.B) and
    quote its main statements as black boxes.
- **SGA 5 II proper base change in higher dimension.** Same status as SGA 4.
- **SGA 7 application to proper smooth surfaces.** Pointer at the end of file 63 (XIII.4.8).
- **Abhyankar's conjecture in characteristic `p`.** Pointer at XIII.2.13; proved by Raynaud / Harbater.
- **Strong desingularization in characteristic `p`.** Pointer to Hironaka in characteristic zero; the characteristic-
    `p` analogue is open. The Künneth formula XIII.4.6 is therefore unconditional in characteristic zero and depends on
    a desingularization hypothesis in characteristic `p`.

#### Forward path.

Reader who has worked through files 01–64 is ready to:

- read **SGA 4** in full (étale topology and étale cohomology, with the gentle SGA series providing the geometric
    intuition for `π_1^ét`, fibre products, descent, NC divisors, and Riemann existence);
- read **SGA 4½** (Deligne, *Cohomologie étale*) and the Weil conjectures;
- read **SGA 5** (cohomologically proper morphisms in dim `> 1`, trace formula, ℓ-adic Lefschetz);
- read **SGA 7** (Murre's surface-`π_1` calculation, vanishing-cycles framework);
- read modern reader's-companion accounts: J. Milne, *Étale Cohomology* (Princeton, 1980); J.-P. Serre's surveys on
    `π_1`; Lenstra, *Galois Theory for Schemes*; Murre / Cadoret, *Galois Categories*; Vistoli, *FGA Explained* (descent
    and stacks); Stacks Project.

#### Final slogan.

**The gentle SGA series builds the étale fundamental group, the descent framework, the analytic comparison, and the
cohomological-properness language from rings and categories up — every theorem stated exactly, every black box named,
every cross-reference to a file in this series. It ends where SGA 4 begins.**
