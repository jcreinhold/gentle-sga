# Exposé IX §IX.5 (gentle): the fundamental group via Seifert–van Kampen

## What this file is about

A connected space has a fundamental group: the group of loops based at a chosen point, up to continuous deformation.
Compute the fundamental group of a sphere or a doughnut and you have a fingerprint of the space's loop structure. In
topology a classical theorem of Seifert and van Kampen shows how to recover this group from a covering of the space by
overlapping patches, together with the gluing on the pairwise overlaps. Each patch contributes its own loops, and the
overlaps impose relations.

This file ports the theorem into algebraic geometry. Given an algebraic space and a suitable covering by simpler pieces,
the fundamental group of the whole is assembled from the fundamental groups of the pieces and of their pairwise
intersections, subject to a list of compatibility relations.

## Opening: from descent to a group presentation

File 45 closed §IX.4 with the **five effectivity criteria**: every universally submersive $g : S' \to S$ is a descent
morphism for étale preschemes (§IX.3), and five sufficient hypotheses on $g$ upgrade descent to effective descent
(§IX.4). The descent isomorphism

```text
ℱ_S  ≃  {(F′, descent datum) : F′ ∈ ℱ_{S′}, descent datum on g : S′ → S}
```

then becomes an isomorphism of categories — for $\mathcal{F}$ the fibred category of finite étale coverings.

Now we cash in. By file 33 V.7, finite étale coverings of a connected prescheme $S$ are classified by the **étale
fundamental group** $\pi_{1}(S, a)$: the category of finite étale coverings is equivalent to the category of finite sets
with continuous $\pi_{1}(S, a)$-action. The descent isomorphism above therefore yields a *group-theoretic* formula
relating $\pi_{1}(S, a)$ to the data:

- $\pi_{s'} = \pi_{1}(S', \bar{s'})$ for $s'$ ranging over the connected components of $S'$,
- $\pi_{s''} = \pi_{1}(S'', \bar{s''})$ for $s''$ ranging over the connected components of $S'' = S' \times_{S} S'$,
- $\pi_{s'''} = \pi_{1}(S''', \bar{s'''})$ for $s'''$ ranging over the connected components of
  $S''' = S' \times_{S} S' \times_{S} S'$,
- the *cocycle 2-isomorphisms* of the descent isomorphism on the fibred category over $S'''$.

The result is **Theorem IX.5.1**: the Seifert–van-Kampen formula for $\pi_{1}(S, a)$. It is the algebraic analogue of
the classical topological statement: the π₁ of a space $B = \bigcup_{i} U_{i}$ is computed from the π₁ of the $U_{i}$
and pairwise intersections $U_{i} \cap U_{j}$ via amalgamated pushouts.

> **Slogan for this file.** *The descent isomorphism for finite étale coverings is a Seifert–van-Kampen formula:
> $\pi_{1}(S, a)$ is the galoisian-type group generated by $\pi_{s'}$ for $s' \in \pi_{0}(S')$ and $g_{s''}$ for
> $s'' \in \pi_{0}(S'')$, subject to cocycle relations on $S'''$.*

## §IX.5 — Translation in terms of the fundamental group

<!-- source: IX.5 -->

### Setup: connected components and base points

<!-- source: IX.5 preamble -->

Throughout this section let $g : S' \to S$ be a morphism of effective descent for the fibred category of finite étale
coverings (e.g. faithfully flat quasi-compact by IX.4.1, finite surjective of finite presentation by IX.4.7, or proper
surjective of finite presentation by IX.4.12). Assume $S$ is **connected**, and assume each of $S$, $S'$,
$S'' = S' \times_{S} S'$, $S''' = S' \times_{S} S' \times_{S} S'$ is a disjoint union of **connected** preschemes. This
holds in particular when each of these schemes is *locally connected* (hence a fortiori when each is *locally
noetherian*; cf. file 33 V.9.1's hypothesis).

Set

```text
E′  = π₀(S′),    E″ = π₀(S″),    E‴ = π₀(S‴),
```

the sets of connected components. The simplicial structure of $S' \times_{S} S' \times_{S} \cdots \times_{S} S'$ (with
face and degeneracy maps given by the projections and the diagonals) descends to a simplicial structure on
$(E', E'', E''')$:

```text
q_1, q_2 :  E″ → E′    (induced from p_1, p_2 : S″ ⇉ S′ by applying π₀)
q_{ij}   :  E‴ → E″    (induced from p_{ij} : S‴ ⇉ S″ for (i, j) ∈ {(2,1), (3,2), (3,1)})
v_1, v_2, v_3 : E‴ → E′ (induced from u_1, u_2, u_3 : S‴ ⇉ S′)
```

The connectedness of $S$ is recorded by $\pi_{0}(K_{\bullet}) = \{*\}$ where $K_{\bullet} = \pi_{0}(S^{\times n+1}_{S})$
is the simplicial set above — i.e. the **equivalence relation on $E'$ generated by the pair $(q_{1}, q_{2})$ is
transitive**.

**Base point choice.** Fix once and for all:

1. An element $s_{0}' \in E'$ (a chosen base connected component of $S'$).
1. For every $s' \in E'$, an element $\bar{s'} \in E''$ such that $q_{1}(\bar{s'}) = s_{0}'$ and $q_{2}(\bar{s'}) = s'$.
   Such $\bar{s'}$ exists because $\pi_{0}(K_{\bullet}) = *$; this *witnesses* the connectedness of $S$. (The source
   observes that $\bar{s'}$ may not exist in every case; if so, the statement of IX.5.1 has to be modified
   appropriately, but the corollaries remain valid.)
1. For each $s' \in E'$, a **geometric point** of the connected component $s' \subset S'$, giving a fibre functor
   $F_{s'} ' : \mathcal{C}(S') \to \mathrm{FSet}$ on the multigaloisian category of finite étale coverings of $S'$. Its
   automorphism group is the **fundamental group of $S'$ at that geometric point**:

$$
\pi_{s'}  =  \pi_{1}(S',  \bar{s'}_{geom}).
$$

Similarly for $s'' \in E''$ and $s''' \in E'''$, giving $\pi_{s''}$ and $\pi_{s'''}$.

**Path classes.** For every $s'' \in E''$, the connected component `p_1(\bar{s″}_{geom}) \subset S′` lies in the same
component as the geometric point $\bar{q_{1}(s'')}_{geom}$, so there is a *path-class isomorphism of fibre functors*

$$
F_{s''}'' \circ p^{*}_{1}  \simeq  F_{q_{1}(s'')}'
$$

— and similarly for $q_{2}$ and for the $q_{ij}$. Choosing all these path classes gives **group homomorphisms**:

```text
q_i^{s″} : π_{s″}  →  π_{q_i(s″)}       (i = 1, 2)
q_{ij}^{s‴} : π_{s‴}  →  π_{q_{ij}(s‴)}    ((i, j) ∈ {(2,1), (3,2), (3,1)})
```

**Cocycle data.** The split fibred-category structure of file 37 (§VI.8) on
$(\mathcal{C}(S'), \mathcal{C}(S''), \mathcal{C}(S'''))$ carries *cocycle 2-isomorphisms* on $S'''$:

```text
p_{21}^* p_1^* ≃ p_{31}^* p_1^*,    p_{21}^* p_2^* ≃ p_{32}^* p_1^*,    p_{31}^* p_2^* ≃ p_{32}^* p_2^*,
```

each deduced from the natural isomorphism with $u^{*}_{i}$ ($i = 1, 2, 3$), the three projections $u_{i} : S''' \to S'$.
Making these data explicit yields, for every $s''' \in E'''$ and every $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, a **well-determined
element**

$$
a^{s'''}_{i}  \in  \pi_{v_{i}(s''')},
$$

subject to compatibility conditions among the $q^{s''}_{i}, q^{s'''}_{ij}$ and the $a^{s'''}_{i}$ (one such condition is
displayed below in the relations c) of IX.5.1).

### IX.5.1 — The main π₁ formula

<!-- source: IX.5.1 -->

**Theorem IX.5.1.** Let $g : S' \to S$ be a morphism of preschemes that is an **effective descent morphism for the
fibred category of finite étale coverings** (e.g. IX.4.9 or IX.4.12). Suppose $S$ is connected and $S', S'', S'''$ are
disjoint sums of connected preschemes. Choose $s_{0}' \in E'$, geometric points in every component, path classes, and
the elements $\bar{s'} \in E''$ as above. Then the **étale fundamental group** of $S$ at the image-base-point of
$s_{0}'$ is canonically isomorphic to

```text
π₁(S, image-base-point of s₀′)  ≃
    Galoisian-type group generated by π_{s′} (s′ ∈ E′) and generators g_{s″} (s″ ∈ E″),
    subject to relations a), b), c) below.
```

The relations:

- **a)** Compatibility with the $\pi_{s''}$ action: for every $s'' \in E''$ and every $g'' \in \pi_{s''}$,

```text
g_{s″} · q_1^{s″}(g″)  =  q_2^{s″}(g″) · g_{s″}.
```

- **b)** Normalisation: for every $s' \in E'$,

$$
g_{\bar{s'}}  =  g_{\bar{s_{0}'}}.
$$

- **c)** The cocycle relation on $S'''$: for every $s''' \in E'''$,

```text
a_3^{s‴}  ·  g_{q_{31}(s‴)}  ·  a_1^{s‴}  =  g_{q_{32}(s‴)}  ·  a_2^{s‴}  ·  g_{q_{21}(s‴)}.
```

*This result says:* **$\pi_{1}(S, a)$ is computed from $\pi_{1}$'s of the connected components of $S'$, with $g_{s''}$
for each connected component of $S''$ (which encodes the "gluing along the cover"), subject to the cocycle relations on
$S'''$ (the "consistency on triple intersections").**

This is the algebraic Seifert–van-Kampen formula.

*Proof of IX.5.1.* This is the assembly of the multigaloisian-category machinery from file 32 §V.5 with the descent
isomorphism. We give the conceptual proof; the bookkeeping is laborious but routine.

**Step 1.** A finite étale $X' \to S'$ is the data of finite sets $X'_{s'}$ ($s' \in E'$) on which $\pi_{s'}$ acts
continuously. (This is the file 32 V.5 description of multigaloisian categories specialised to $\mathcal{C}(S')$.)

**Step 2.** A descent datum on $X'$ is the data of bijections $\phi_{s''} : X'_{q_{1}(s'')} \simeq X'_{q_{2}(s'')}$ for
each $s'' \in E''$, compatible with the actions of $\pi_{s''}$ through $q^{s''}_{i}$. (Geometrically: the descent datum
is an isomorphism $p^{*}_{1}(X') \to p^{*}_{2}(X')$ over $S''$.)

**Step 3.** Choose the special $s'' \in E''$ of the form $\bar{s'}$ (with $q_{1}(\bar{s'}) = s_{0}'$,
$q_{2}(\bar{s'}) = s'$). The bijection $\phi_{\bar{s'}} : X'_{s_{0}'} \simeq X'_{s'}$ lets us **identify** every
$X'_{s'}$ with the same set $F_{0}'(X') = X'_{s_{0}'}$. After this identification, the $\pi_{s'}$ all act on
$F_{0}'(X')$.

**Step 4.** The other $\phi_{s''}$ correspond to bijections $g_{s''} : F_{0}'(X') \to F_{0}'(X')$, subject to:

- compatibility with $\pi_{s''}$ (relation a) above);
- compatibility with the identification of step 3 (relation b) above);
- the cocycle property on $S'''$ (relation c) above).

**Step 5.** This builds an equivalence between (descent data on finite étale coverings of $S'$) and (finite sets with
the action of all $\pi_{s'}$ plus bijections $g_{s''}$ satisfying a), b), c)).

**Step 6.** Let $G$ be the free group generated by the union of $\pi_{s'}$ and $\{g_{s''} : s'' \in E''\}$, modulo
relations a), b), c). Let $\pi$ be the inverse limit of the quotients of $G$ by subgroups of finite index whose inverse
images in each $\pi_{s'}$ are open. Then the category in step 5 is equivalent to the category of finite sets with
continuous $\pi$-action.

**Step 7.** Since $g$ is an effective descent morphism for finite étale coverings, the category in step 5 is also
equivalent to $\mathcal{C}(S)$ (finite étale coverings of $S$). Hence

```text
𝒞(S)  ≃  𝒞(π) = continuous π-sets.
```

By file 33 V.7.4, this forces $\pi_{1}(S, a) \simeq \pi$ (canonically). ∎

> **Slogan.** *The Seifert–van-Kampen formula is the descent isomorphism for finite étale coverings, translated into
> group theory via Galois categories.*

### IX.5.2 — Topological finite generation

<!-- source: IX.5.2 -->

**Corollary IX.5.2.** Suppose $E' = \pi_{0}(S')$ and $E'' = \pi_{0}(S'')$ are both **finite**, and the fundamental
groups $\pi_{s'}$ are all **topologically finitely generated**. Then $\pi_{1}(S, a)$ is topologically finitely
generated.

*Proof.* The galoisian-type group in IX.5.1 has finite generating set: the union of finite generating sets for each
$\pi_{s'}$ ($s' \in E'$, finite) plus generators $g_{s''}$ ($s'' \in E''$, finite). ∎

This is the technical engine of the (open) question whether $\pi_{1}(X, x)$ is topologically finitely generated for $X$
proper smooth over an algebraically closed field. As Grothendieck notes (file 09, page 248):

> Thus we shall prove later that the fundamental group of a normal projective scheme over an algebraically closed field
> is topologically finitely generated. Using Chow's lemma and normalisation of algebraic schemes, it will follow that
> the same result is true for every proper scheme over an algebraically closed field.

### IX.5.3 — Topological finite presentation

<!-- source: IX.5.3 -->

**Corollary IX.5.3.** Suppose $E', E'', E'''$ are all **finite**, the fundamental groups $\pi_{s'}$ are topologically
**finitely presented**, and the fundamental groups $\pi_{s''}$ are topologically **finitely generated**. Then
$\pi_{1}(S, a)$ is topologically **finitely presented**.

*Proof.* The relations a), b), c) of IX.5.1 are finitely many in this situation. ∎

This is sharper than IX.5.2: it bounds the number of relations as well as generators.

### IX.5.4 — Pinching along a discrete set of points

<!-- source: IX.5.4 -->

**Corollary IX.5.4 (Pinching).** Let $g : S' \to S$ be a finite morphism of finite presentation, and let $T \subset S$
be a **discrete** subset. For every $s \in S$, let $n(s)$ be the **geometric number of points** in the fibre $g^{-1}(s)$
(equivalently, the separable degree of $g^{-1}(s)$ over $\kappa(s)$, summed over residue field extensions). Suppose that
**$n(s) = 1$ for every $s \in S - T$**: outside $T$, $g$ is "geometrically a single-point cover," i.e. radicial
surjective.

For every $s \in T$, let $K_{s}$ be an algebraically closed extension of $\kappa(s)$, let $I_{s}$ be the set of
geometric points of $S'$ with values in $K_{s}$ (a set of cardinality $n(s)$), let $I_{s}'$ be $I_{s}$ minus one chosen
base point, and let $I' = \amalg_{s \in T} I_{s}'$. Suppose $S'$ is **connected**.

Then the fundamental group of $S$ is

```text
π₁(S, a)  ≃  galoisian-type group generated by π₁(S′, a′) and \{g_i : i ∈ I′\},
                                    subject to no further relations.
```

*This result says:* **pinching $S'$ along the $n(s) - 1$ "extra" geometric points over each $s \in T$ adds one free
generator per identified geometric point.** The total number of new generators is $\sum_{s \in T} (n(s) - 1)$.

*Proof sketch.* The hypotheses force $S'' = S' \times_{S} S'$ and $S''' = S' \times_{S} S' \times_{S} S'$ to have
specific simple descriptions:

- $S'' = S' \amalg Z_{2}$ where $Z_{2} \to S$ is concentrated over $T$ and counts ordered pairs of distinct geometric
  points of $g^{-1}(t)$ for each $t \in T$.
- $S''' = S' \amalg Z_{3}$ where $Z_{3} \to S$ is concentrated over $T$ and counts ordered triples of distinct geometric
  points.

The fundamental groups of the `Z_2` and `Z_3` components are *trivial* (they are spectra of algebraically closed
fields), so relations a) and c) of IX.5.1 trivialise. Relation b) and step 3 of IX.5.1's proof reduce to free
identification of the generators $g_{s''}$ for each $s''$ in the `Z_2` part of $E''$, indexed by $I'$. ∎

> **Slogan.** *Pinching a scheme along a finite set of geometric points adds one free generator per identified geometric
> point.*

### IX.5.5 — Double-point curve: $\pi_{1} = \hat{\mathbb{Z}}$

<!-- source: IX.5.5 -->

**Example IX.5.5 (Nodal cubic).** Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field, and let $S$ be a complete rational curve
over $k$ with **exactly one ordinary double point**: e.g. the nodal cubic $y^{2} = x^{3} + x^{2}$ in
$\mathbb{P}^{2}_{k}$. Set $S' = \mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}$, the normalisation of $S$, with normalisation map $g : S' \to S$
finite and surjective. The morphism $g$ is a homeomorphism outside the node, and at the node $g^{-1}(node)$ consists of
**two distinct geometric points** (the two branches of the curve at the node).

Apply IX.5.4 with `T = \{node\}` and $n(node) = 2$. Then $I_{node} = 2$ points, $I_{node}' = 1$ point, so $I'$ has
exactly **one** generator. The fundamental group $\pi_{1}(S', a') = \pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}, a') = (1)$ (the
projective line over an algebraically closed field is simply connected, cf. file 12 closing remark for the classical
proof, also stated inside file 33 V.9 as an example). Hence

```text
π₁(S, a)  ≃  galoisian-type group on one free generator  =  ℤ̂.
```

> **Slogan.** *A complete rational curve with one ordinary double point has the étale fundamental group
> $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ of the punctured affine line.*

More generally, a complete rational curve with $n$ ordinary double points has $\pi_{1} =$ free profinite group on $n$
generators.

**Unibranch remark.** A *cusp* (or more generally a *geometrically unibranch* point) of a complete rational curve has
$n(cusp) = 1$, so IX.5.4 adds no new generator at the cusp. The étale fundamental group of a complete rational curve
with cuspidal singularities is the same as that of its normalisation $\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}$ — namely trivial.

### IX.5.6 — Geometrically connected fibres ⇒ cokernel form

<!-- source: IX.5.6 -->

**Corollary IX.5.6.** Let $g : S' \to S$ be **universally submersive** with **geometrically connected fibres** (cf.
IX.3.4), with $S$ connected. Then $S'$ is connected, and (choosing a geometric point $s'$ in $S'$ and a base point
$s \in S$ for $g$):

```text
π₁(S, s)   ≃   cokernel of  (p_1^*, p_2^*) : π₁(S″, s″) ⇉ π₁(S′, s′)  in Galois-type groups
```

where $s'' = diag(s')$ is the geometric diagonal of $s'$ in $S''$. Equivalently: $\pi_{1}(S, s)$ is the quotient of
$\pi_{1}(S', s')$ by the closed normal subgroup generated by the elements

```text
p_1^*(g″) · p_2^*(g″)^{-1}    for g″ ∈ π₁(S″, s″).
```

If $g$ is moreover a morphism of **effective descent** for finite étale coverings (e.g. IX.4.12 if $g$ is proper
surjective of finite presentation), this is the *full* fundamental group.

*Proof.* By IX.3.4 (file 45), the descent functor $g^{*} : \mathcal{C}(S) \to \mathcal{C}(S')$ is fully faithful, hence
the homomorphism on fundamental groups is an *epi* (file 32 V.6.9 + V.7.5). Geometric connectedness of fibres implies
$E' = \pi_{0}(S') = \{*\}$ and $E'' = \pi_{0}(S'') = \{diagonal\}$ since each fibre of $g$ is connected and so each
fibre of $S'' \to S$ is also connected. So the IX.5.1 formula collapses: there is *one* generator $g_{diag}$ from
$S'' = \{diag\}$, *one* constraint from the path-class identification (relation b)), and the *commutation* a) becomes
exactly the cokernel description. ∎

> **Slogan.** *Geometrically connected fibres turn the Seifert–van-Kampen formula into a clean cokernel: $\pi_{1}(S)$ is
> the coequaliser of `π₁(S″) ⇉ π₁(S′)`.*

### IX.5.7 — Remark on finite presentation in characteristic $p$

<!-- source: IX.5.7 -->

**Remark IX.5.7.** It is **not known** whether the fundamental group of a proper scheme over an algebraically closed
field is topologically finitely presented. The source (page 250) flags this:

> It is not known at present whether the fundamental group of a proper scheme over an algebraically closed field $k$ is
> topologically finitely presented. This seems very unlikely for smooth curves of genus $g \geq 2$ in characteristic
> $p > 0$; for the largest prime-to-$p$ quotient, however, known techniques seem to give an affirmative answer.

In characteristic `0`, using *transcendental methods* (comparison with the topological fundamental group via Riemann's
existence theorem and Lefschetz's theorem on Stein spaces), the answer is *yes* for proper schemes over $\mathbb{C}$,
and a fortiori over any algebraically closed field of characteristic `0`. In characteristic $p > 0$, the question
remains open: smooth curves of genus $g \geq 2$ have larger fundamental group than their characteristic-`0` counterparts
(wild ramification phenomena at the cusps at infinity), and finite-presentation results for these groups are not known.

This is one of the deeper open questions of arithmetic geometry. The local *p*-adic side connects to **Mochizuki's work
on the anabelian fundamental group** and the theory of **temperate** vs. **profinite** fundamental groups.

### IX.5.8 — Quotient by a finite group $\Gamma$

<!-- source: IX.5.8 -->

**Theorem IX.5.8 (Quotient by a finite group).** Let $S'$ be a prescheme, let $\Gamma$ be a finite group acting on $S'$
by automorphisms (compatibly with the structural morphism to a base, or just over $\mathbf{Spec}\,\mathbb{Z}$ if no base
is fixed), and let $S = S' / \Gamma$ be the quotient scheme (file 30 V.1.2). Then the **category of finite étale
coverings of $S$** is equivalent to the **category of finite étale coverings $X'$ of $S'$ equipped with a compatible
$\Gamma$-action**, such that for every $s' \in S'$ and every $g \in \Gamma_{s'}$ (where $\Gamma_{s'}$ is the **inertia
group** of $s'$ in $\Gamma$; see file 30 V.2.3), $g$ **acts trivially on the fibre $X'_{s'}$**.

The proof is a direct calculation using IX.5.1. Specifically: in the category $\mathcal{C}_{0}'$ of finite étale
coverings of $S'$ with **compatible $\Gamma$-action** (without the inertia restriction), the natural fibre functor
$X' \mapsto X'_{a'}$ is fundamental (file 32 V.5.7), giving a Galois category with automorphism group
$G = \pi_{1}(S', \Gamma; a')$. There is a canonical exact sequence

```text
1  →  π₁(S′, a′)  →  G  →  Γ  →  1.
```

Moreover for every geometric point $b'$ of $S'$, the inertia group $\Gamma_{b'} \subset \Gamma$ lifts to a homomorphism

$$
u_{b'} : \Gamma_{b'}  \to  G,
$$

defined up to inner automorphism by $\pi_{1}(S', a')$, whose composite with $G \to \Gamma$ is the inclusion
$\Gamma_{b'} \to \Gamma$.

Under these identifications, **$\pi_{1}(S, a)$ is canonically isomorphic to the quotient group of $G$ by the closed
normal subgroup generated by the images of the inertia homomorphisms $u_{b'}$ over all geometric points $b'$**.

In particular: the image of $\pi_{1}(S', a')$ in $\pi_{1}(S, a)$ is a *normal* subgroup, with quotient a quotient of
$\Gamma$.

*Reduction of the number of relations.* For every $g \in \Gamma$ ($g \neq e$), the subprescheme $S'_{g} \subset S'$
where $id_{S'}$ and $g$ agree (the *fixed-point locus* of $g$) is a closed subscheme. Choose a geometric point
$b'_{g,i}$ in each connected component of $S'_{g}$, and a lift $\bar{g}_{i} \in G$ of $g$ for each. **The quotient of
$G$ by the closed normal subgroup generated by all the $\bar{g}_{i}$ is $\pi_{1}(S, a)$.**

*Semidirect product when $a'$ is $\Gamma$-fixed.* If $a'$ is a geometric point of $S'$ fixed by $\Gamma$, then $\Gamma$
acts naturally on $\pi_{1}(S', a')$, and $G$ is the corresponding **semidirect product**

```text
G  ≃  π₁(S′, a′) ⋊ Γ.
```

Identifying $\Gamma$ with a subgroup of $G$, the relations "$g = e$" for $g \in \Gamma_{a'}$ (the inertia at $a'$) are
forced by the choice of base point. Thus:

> **Slogan.** *If $a'$ is fixed by $\Gamma$, then $\pi_{1}(S, a)$ is the quotient of `π₁(S′, a′) ⋊ Γ` by the relations
> $\bar{g}_{i} = e$ for the connected-component representatives of the fixed-point loci $S'_{g}$.*

**Application: symmetric powers.** Take $S' = X^{n}$ (the $n$-th cartesian power of a connected proper scheme $X$ over
an algebraically closed field $k$), and let $\Gamma = \mathfrak{S}_{n}$ (the symmetric group on $n$ letters) act by
permuting factors. The quotient is the **$n$-th symmetric power** $Sym^{n}(X) = X^{n} / \mathfrak{S}_{n}$.

Take $a'$ to be a geometric point **on the diagonal** $\Delta : X \hookrightarrow X^{n}$. Then
$\Gamma_{a'} = \mathfrak{S}_{n}$ (the full symmetric group fixes the diagonal). The inertia loci $(X^{n})_{g}$ for
$g \in \mathfrak{S}_{n}$ are products of diagonals of various dimensions, all of which **contain the diagonal
$\Delta$**. So every inertia locus passes through the chosen base point $a'$, and IX.5.8 gives:

```text
π₁(Sym^n X, a)  ≃  quotient of π₁(X^n, a′) ⋊ 𝔖_n  by  the inertia relations,
                 ≃  quotient of π₁(X^n, a′) by the closed normal subgroup generated by Γ-orbit relations.
```

The result of the next exposé (Exposé X, deferred from the gentle SGA pass) shows that for $X$ proper and connected over
an algebraically closed field, $\pi_{1}(X^{n}) \simeq \pi_{1}(X)^{n}$. Plugging this in: $\pi_{1}(X^{n})$ is the
$n$-fold product of $\pi_{1}(X)$, and the $\mathfrak{S}_{n}$-action permutes factors. Taking the quotient by the
relations from inertia at the diagonal *identifies* the $n$ factors, giving:

```text
π₁(Sym^n X, a)  ≃  π₁(X)^{ab}  (the abelianisation of π₁(X)),  for n ≥ 2.
```

> **Slogan.** *For $X$ proper connected over $\bar{k}$ (any characteristic) and $n \geq 2$, the fundamental group of the
> $n$-th symmetric power is the abelianisation of the fundamental group of $X$.*

**Closing aside: the Jacobian.** Take $X$ a smooth projective curve over $\bar{k}$, and $n \geq 2g - 1$ where $g$ is the
genus. Then $Sym^{n}(X)$ is a projective bundle over the Jacobian $J(X)$ (this is *Abel's theorem*, file XI of SGA 1 in
spirit), and projective bundles have the *same* fundamental group as their base. Combined with the symmetric-power
result above:

```text
π₁(J(X))  ≃  π₁(Sym^n X)  ≃  π₁(X)^{ab}.
```

This is the algebraic-geometry analogue of the classical fact that **the first homology of an algebraic curve is the
same as that of its Jacobian** — but it now holds for the étale $\pi_{1}$ in *any characteristic*. In characteristic
`0`, comparison with the topological $\pi^{top}_{1}$ recovers the classical Riemann-surface statement. In characteristic
$p$, this gives a non-trivial statement about étale fundamental groups in positive characteristic.

> **Slogan for IX.5.8.** *A finite quotient is the right kind of descent. The Seifert–van-Kampen formula computes
> $\pi_{1}(S' / \Gamma)$ from $\pi_{1}(S')$ and the inertia data.*

## Closing pointer to file 47

The Seifert–van-Kampen formula of §IX.5 computes $\pi_{1}(S, a)$ from $\pi_{s'}, \pi_{s''}$ and the cocycle. The
corollary IX.5.6 — for $g$ with geometrically connected fibres — turns it into a clean cokernel formula. File 47 (§IX.6)
takes the further geometric case where $g = f : X \to S$ is *proper surjective with geometrically connected fibres*, and
applies the cokernel formula to prove the **fundamental exact sequence**

```text
1  →  π₁(X̄_0, ā)  →  π₁(X, a)  →  π₁(S, b)  →  1
```

— the algebraic analogue of the homotopy exact sequence of a topological fibre bundle. The proof uses **Stein
factorisation** (a new black box from EGA III §4.3), the topological invariance theorem **IX.4.10** of file 45, and the
descent theorem **IX.4.12** for proper surjective.

File 47 then closes the gentle SGA project.
